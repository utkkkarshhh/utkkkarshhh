Research Statement & Technical Inquiries

Investigating resilient abstractions across distributed state machines, asynchronous I/O multiplexers, and decentralized cryptographic verification. Prepared for graduate research (MS / Ph.D.) admissions and collaborative labs.

RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY TARGET: MS / PH.D. ADMISSIONS

1. Systems Under Contention: Scaling Asynchronous & Distributed Primitives

Modern hyperscale computing is undergoing a structural shift. As single-core clock frequencies plateau and heterogeneous architectures dominate, software throughput is fundamentally bounded by concurrency overhead, lock contention, and network I/O tail latency. In production distributed backends, a single slow RPC or a blocked coroutine frame can propagate cascading backpressure through an entire service topology, causing throughput collapse.

My engineering experience at Agbe Technologies with high-concurrency asynchronous microservices (FastAPI, ASGI event loops, PostgreSQL indexing, and distributed caching) has shown me that abstract algorithms must be rigorously coupled with empirical systems mechanics. I am drawn to graduate research to explore foundational questions:

[Q1] Event Loop Starvation & Hybrid Workloads

How can single-threaded asynchronous event loops (e.g., Python asyncio, Node.js libuv) dynamically detect and offload compute-heavy coroutines before they breach epoll latency SLA thresholds, without incurring excessive inter-thread context-switch jitter?

[Q2] Consensus Latency Under Partial Churn

What hybrid state-machine replication protocols can achieve near-linear commit latencies during stable network intervals while guaranteeing safety under arbitrary Byzantine or fail-stop partitions?

2. Verifiable Computing & Decentralized Trust

My work on projects like the Decentralized Election DApp and Ghost Mail demonstrated the friction between privacy, auditability, and gas/computational efficiency. In permissionless distributed ledgers, verifying state transitions requires cryptographic invariants that often bloat storage or latency. I am eager to study how recursive zero-knowledge proofs and succinct cryptographic commitments can be embedded into high-throughput distributed database backends.
